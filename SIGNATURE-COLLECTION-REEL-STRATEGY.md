## 0. The strategic reframe

Don't treat this as "2 real basins + 8 fake ones." Treat it as **one continuous shoot that happens to be split across two capture methods** — real camera on real stone, and virtual camera on real photography. The two physical basins aren't just products #1 and #2; they are the **reference standard** that every other basin in the campaign gets measured against, visually. If Carrara and Travertine look expensive, tactile, and specific, the audience extends that trust forward to the stills automatically — *as long as nothing about the visual grammar changes between them.*

So the system below is built around one rule: **build the world once, then populate it with 10 products.** Same backdrop, same light, same lens behaviour, same grain, same typography, same sound. The join between "filmed" and "animated" should be invisible not because the AI is undetectable, but because you never give the eye a reason to compare them side by side in a way that invites scrutiny.

## 1. The Mother Shoot — exact shot list for Carrara & Travertine

Shoot both basins in the same session, same set, same lighting rig, back to back. This is the single most important production day of the whole campaign — everything else (reel formats, motion-matching on the stills, B-roll for transitions) is derived from this footage. Budget a full day.

**Set:** matte black or deep charcoal backdrop/table (seamless), single black-painted plinth or riser at basin height. No visible bathroom fittings — treat it like a jewellery/watch shoot, not a showroom photo.

**Lighting base:** one key softbox at 35–45° above, warm-neutral (~3200–3500K), large and diffused so it wraps the stone rather than hotspotting it; one small hard rim/kicker light from behind-side (cooler, ~5600K) to separate the basin edge from the black background and catch the polish; negative fill (black flag) on the opposite side to keep shadows deep. This exact lighting setup is what you'll replicate virtually on the 8 stills, so photograph/video reference frames of the empty set too.

## 2. Animating the 8 remaining stills — method and rules

**Principle:** the AI is a *lighting and camera operator*, never a *sculptor*. It is only ever allowed to move light, shadow, atmosphere, or a virtual camera around a basin whose geometry is treated as fixed. It is not allowed to invent new geometry, change proportions, or "complete" an angle that doesn't exist in the source photo.

Techniques, ranked by safety (safest first):

1. **Static camera, moving light/shadow** (safest). The still doesn't move at all — only a light source or shadow sweeps across it, exactly matching shot #11/#12 from the Mother Shoot. Because the camera never moves, there's nothing for the AI to get wrong about geometry — it's animating illumination, not form. This should be your default technique for most of the 8 stills.
2. **Extremely slow push or pull** (5–10% scale change over 3–4 seconds, dead-straight axis, no rotation). Kept short and subtle, this reads as intentional cinematography rather than "AI trying to move a camera around a 3D object it doesn't understand."
3. **Subtle parallax/orbit, ≤5–8°.** Only attempt this on stills with a clean, simple background (easiest for the model to separate basin from environment) and always cap the arc small enough that the model never needs to render a side of the basin it can't see in the source.
4. **Product emerging from darkness** — start frame is the still crushed to near-black, light "arrives" over 1–2 seconds to reveal the full, already-correct still. This is really a light/exposure animation, not a geometry one, so it's very safe and very cinematic.
5. **Macro-to-product transitions** — animate only a cropped detail region (a vein pattern, a rim edge) with a very slow drift, then hard-cut (not morph) to the full still. Cropped macro regions are low-risk because there's no silhouette to distort.
6. **Environmental reflection/atmosphere movement** — add drifting dust/light-flare/particle atmosphere in front of a static basin. Zero risk to the product, adds production value.

**Avoid:** basin rotating in place, camera arcing more than ~10°, any prompt language implying the object itself moves or changes, multi-generation chains (video-of-a-video), and long single generations — the longer the clip, the more the model drifts from the source frame. Cap every individual generation at 2–4 seconds and cut between them rather than relying on one continuous 8-second AI shot.

**Prompting formula (tool-agnostic):**
> "Static product photograph, [basin name/stone description]. Camera is [locked-off / on an extremely slow linear push]. Only the lighting moves: a soft light source sweeps slowly across the stone surface from left to right, shadows shift accordingly. Product does not move, rotate, or deform. No morphing. No new angles. Studio black background, shallow depth of field, cinematic, subtle."

Negative prompt / avoid-list where supported: *warping, morphing, distortion, extra objects, changing shape, rotating object, deforming geometry, unrealistic reflections, cartoon, plastic look.*

**QA every generated clip against the source still**, frame by frame at start and end: rim thickness, vein pattern, drain position, bowl depth, silhouette. If anything drifts, regenerate at a shorter duration or fall back to technique #1 (light-only) — it's the one method that structurally can't get the geometry wrong.

## 3. Five repeatable Reel formats

Build each of these once as an edit template (same project file, same typography/sound layers) so producing basin 03–10 becomes "drop in new footage," not "invent a new concept."

**A — The Reveal** (launch-cadence format, use for each numbered drop)
Darkness → light arrives → full basin → number/name stamp. ~7–10s. Works with real footage (Carrara, Travertine) or a still animated with technique #4 above. This is your primary "01, 02, 03…" drop format — the one thing that stays completely consistent across all 10 releases so the audience recognises the ritual.

**B — Light Study**
No camera movement at all. Basin sits still, a spotlight or shadow travels across the surface for the full duration. ~8–12s. The most forgiving format for the 8 animated stills (technique #1) because there's zero geometry risk — use this generously for basins 03–10.

**C — Slab to Basin**
Opens on raw stone slab/quarry-style texture or an offcut (real footage from the Mother Shoot, shot #22), then a hard cut or graphic wipe to the finished basin in the same lighting. Tells the material-origin story and gives you a reason to use real, non-basin stone footage as connective tissue between products. ~10–12s.

**D — Hand & Water**
Built entirely from real macro/tactile footage of Carrara and Travertine (shots 13–18). No AI at all. Rotates through different detail crops over time, cut to sound design. This format's job is to *prove realness* — run it periodically through the campaign (not tied to a specific basin number) to keep re-establishing "this is a real object" trust that then extends to the animated Reels sitting either side of it in the feed.

**E — Ten in One**
A rapid-cut recap once several basins are live: 0.6–1s per basin, same light-sweep motion on every one, ending on the full collection number. Use as a mid-campaign or finale post once you have 5+ basins revealed — reinforces scale and range in one hit.

Rotate: launch day = A, day+2/3 = B or C for the same basin, occasional D as a trust-reset, E as a milestone recap.

## 4. Making real and AI footage belong to the same world

This is the actual craft of the campaign. Lock every one of these before cutting a single Reel:

- **Environment:** matte black/charcoal set, identical falloff, in both filmed and animated shots (match against your empty-frame plates from the Mother Shoot).
- **Light:** same colour temperature pairing (warm ~3200K key, cool rim), same softness quality, same movement *speed* — the shadow sweep on an animated still should travel at the same rate you shot it live on Carrara.
- **Lens language:** shallow depth of field, mild compression (as if shot on an 85mm-equivalent), consistent across both — apply as a look in edit if a still doesn't already read this way.
- **Grain:** apply one uniform, subtle film grain/texture pass over the *entire* edit — real and AI footage alike, in the same edit pass, not per-clip. This does two jobs at once: it unifies the two capture methods under one texture, and it's the single most effective way to disguise the slightly-too-clean quality AI video tends to have.
- **Edit pace:** slow, deliberate — roughly one cut every 1.5–2.5 seconds across all formats. A hurried edit invites scrutiny; a slow one reads as confidence.
- **Typography:** one system for the whole campaign — thin serif or grotesk, small caps, basin number + stone name as a lower-third, same entrance animation (simple fade/slide, nothing flashy) every time.
- **Sound:** a recurring sonic signature — low sub drone bed + real stone/water foley (pulled from your Carrara/Travertine session) + one designed "tone" hit on every reveal moment, same tone across all 10 basins. This is arguably your cheapest and most powerful unifier: audience ears can't tell real footage from animated stills, but they can absolutely tell if the sound design is inconsistent.

**Sequencing rule:** never open a Reel on an animated still. Open on real footage, a hand, water, or macro stone texture — something unmistakably physical — then let the still-based shots sit inside that established world. The real footage "pays" for trust; the animated stills spend it. Format D (Hand & Water) is your trust-reserve — run it whenever you feel the balance tipping too far toward animated content.

## 5. Product-accuracy protocol

Before publishing any AI-assisted clip, check it against the source still and, where possible, the physical spec sheet:

- Rim thickness unchanged start-to-end frame
- Stone veining pattern identical, not "similar" — same vein positions
- Drain position unchanged
- Bowl depth/proportions unchanged
- Silhouette (outer profile) unchanged
- No new angle shown beyond what the source photography actually captured
- Clip length ≤ 3–4 seconds per generation (longer = more drift)
- Prompt describes camera/light motion only — never "the basin rotates," always "the camera orbits" or "the light sweeps"

If a clip fails any check, don't try to fix it with more prompting — regenerate shorter, or fall back to the static-camera/moving-light technique, which structurally cannot fail this checklist.

## 6. Recommended workflow — kept deliberately lean

**Filming (Mother Shoot):** one hired photographer/videographer (or a good mirrorless + macro lens + slider + a 2–3 light kit if DIY) for one day, using the shot list in §1. This single day is what makes the rest of the system possible — don't compress it.

**Image-to-video for the 8 stills:** pick **one** primary tool rather than juggling several subscriptions.
- **Runway (Gen-4 / Gen-3 Alpha Turbo), Image-to-Video mode** is the recommended default — it keeps first-frame product fidelity well and offers explicit camera controls (push/orbit/pan sliders) that map directly onto the motion vocabulary in §1 and §2, and its short-generation workflow suits the 2–4 second clip rule above.
- Keep **Kling** or **Luma Ray2** as a fallback only, for the rare still where Runway struggles with a specific stone's reflectivity or colour — don't build the workflow around switching tools.
- Skip anything marketed as "AI reimagines your product" — you specifically need image-to-video with strong source-frame anchoring, not generative reinterpretation.

**Editing:** DaVinci Resolve or Premiere/CapCut Pro, built as **one reusable master template project** — grain LUT, vignette, typography animation, and the sound bed already in place. Producing basin 03 through 10 becomes: drop clip in, adjust name/number, export.

## First Reel — storyboard

**Recommendation:** open the whole campaign on **01 — White Carrara**, shot entirely from real footage, using **Format A (The Reveal)**. No AI in this one at all. It's your strongest, safest, most universally legible stone, it matches the static-post drop order (reinforcing the "01" ritual from day one), and it lets the very first thing anyone sees from this collection be undeniably, tactilely real — establishing the trust that every later, partly-animated Reel will draw on. Target length: **9 seconds.**

| Time | Shot | Source (Mother Shoot #) | Sound |
|------|------|--------------------------|-------|
| 0:00–0:01.2 | Black frame. Faint text fades in, small caps, centered: "THE SIGNATURE COLLECTION" | — | Low sub drone begins, near-silent |
| 0:01.2–0:03.2 | Extreme macro on Carrara veining, static, focus pull settles | #9 | Drone continues, a single low stone-tap foley hit |
| 0:03.2–0:05.5 | Slow spotlight sweep across full basin, medium framing, shadow rolling across the rim | #11/#12 | Drone swells slightly, water drop foley enters |

This becomes the reference edit for Format A on every subsequent basin: same beat structure, same 9-second target, same tone hit, new stone.

## Second Reel — Beige Travertine (02)

Carrara established the campaign's *skeleton* — Format A's beat structure, the black-set language, the typography and sound system. Travertine now proves the skeleton can carry a different emotional register without becoming a different campaign. **Carrara = elegance, purity, form. Travertine = warmth, texture, material.** The two Reels should feel like two movements of the same piece, not two different pieces.

**The core differentiation strategy:** Carrara sells itself through *light on a mirror-polished surface and cool water* — it's about optical purity. Travertine has no mirror polish to sell — its story is tactile and porous, so it's sold through *grazing light raking across texture and a human hand*, not water. Where Carrara's camera stayed locked-off and let light do the moving, Travertine's camera itself moves (a slow arc/orbit) — because texture only fully reads as you change angle relative to it, the way it does when you turn a real piece of travertine in your hands. That single grammar swap — **static camera + moving light (Carrara)** vs **moving camera + raking light (Travertine)** — is the whole differentiation engine, and it's legible without the two ever needing to look inconsistent, because both are still slow, both are still on the same black set, both still end on the same signature tone hit.

Target length: **10 seconds.**

| Time | Shot | Mother Shoot ref | Camera | Lighting | Sound |
|------|------|-------------------|--------|----------|-------|
| 0:00–0:01.3 | Black frame, but not pure black — the faintest warm glow already breathing at the edges of frame. Text fades in, small caps, centered: "THE SIGNATURE COLLECTION" | — | Static | A whisper of warm (~2800–3000K) light already present, almost imperceptible | Same low sub drone as Reel 01, but with a faint grain/sand texture already audible underneath from frame one — warmth signalled before anything is seen |
| 0:01.3–0:03.5 | Extreme macro on travertine's natural pitting/porous texture. Unlike Carrara's macro (which sat under still light), a soft grazing light is already sweeping across this frame from the first visible moment | #9 (travertine pass) + #12 combined | Static macro | Warm, hard-ish grazing side light sweeping slowly left→right, deliberately low-angle so it catches every pore and shadow-fills the holes | Drone continues, sand/stone grain foley builds in |
| 0:03.5–0:05.5 | A hand enters frame, fingertip traces slowly across the porous rim — Travertine's signature emotional beat, replacing Carrara's water droplet | #17 (texture-tracing take) | Static macro-medium | Same grazing warm light, now catching the hand's shadow as it moves | Soft skin-on-stone friction foley, drone swells underneath |
| 0:05.5–0:08.0 | Camera arcs slowly around the basin (~20–25° partial orbit), medium distance — this is where the **complete basin first appears**, revealed through movement rather than a cut | #2 (partial arc, speed-matched to the turntable pass) | Slow arc/orbit, motorized slider | Warm key rakes across the whole form as the angle changes, texture visibly shifting with the camera rather than the light alone | Drone continues, tonal swell begins building, grain foley fades out |
| 0:08.0–0:10.0 | Arc settles to a stop at the 3/4 hero angle, basin held static and fully visible for the remainder of the reel | #1 (hero angle, matched framing to Carrara's) | Static (arc has settled) | Warm key holds, soft rim kicker separates the edge from black | Signature tone hit lands as the arc settles — same tone/instrument as Carrara's, arriving out of stillness rather than out of a ripple |
| 0:08.8–0:10.0 | Text stamp overlays, bottom third, fades in: "02 — BEIGE TRAVERTINE" + wordmark | — | — | Tone hit sustains, drone fades out |

**When the complete basin appears:** from 0:05.5 through to the end (4.5 of the 10 seconds) — deliberately more full-basin screen time than Carrara's ~1.8 seconds, because texture needs duration and changing angle to register, where Carrara's polish read instantly in a single static hero frame. Both Reels satisfy the "clearly show the complete basin" requirement; they just spend different amounts of time doing it, for a reason tied to the material itself.

**Transition logic:** hard cuts only, no dissolves — identical cut language to Carrara. The beat skeleton is now locked as: *opening card → macro texture/light → tactile human beat → camera reveal of full basin → hero stop + tone hit + stamp.* That five-beat skeleton, not the specific content of each beat, is what Format A now means — basin 03 onward inherits this skeleton and varies only the texture, light quality, and touch/water choice to match each stone's character.

**Typography:** identical system and animation (same font, same fade/slide-in, same small-caps treatment) to Carrara — only the timing shifts to sit ~0.6–0.8 seconds after the tone hit in both Reels, preserving the felt rhythm of "beat lands, then the name confirms it" even though this Reel runs a second longer overall.

**How it differs from Carrara, without breaking consistency:**

| | 01 Carrara | 02 Travertine |
|---|---|---|
| Emotional register | Elegance, purity, form | Warmth, texture, material |
| Camera | Locked-off throughout | Slow arc/orbit for the reveal |
| Light behaviour | Introduced mid-reel as a discrete sweep | Present and moving from the first frame |
| Colour temperature | Cooler, higher-contrast, polish-forward | Warmer (~2800–3000K), softer contrast, texture-forward |
| Signature sensory beat | Water droplet + ripple | Hand tracing texture |
| Full-basin reveal mechanism | Static pull-back cut | Camera movement (orbit) settling to stillness |
| Full-basin screen time | ~1.8s | ~4.5s |
| Constant across both | Black set, cut-only editing, typography system, signature tone hit, ~9–10s runtime, five-beat Format A skeleton | |

Once Carrara and Travertine are both locked as finished edits, they become the **reference pair** for testing the real→AI system on 03 Nero Marquina: Nero should borrow Carrara's grammar (cool, mirror-polish, purity) rather than Travertine's (texture/movement), since Nero Marquina is itself a polished, high-contrast stone — which is exactly the kind of "which real Reel does this still's material logic belong to" decision this pair now exists to make repeatable for basins 04–10 too.
