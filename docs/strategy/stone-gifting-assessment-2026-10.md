# Prime Piece: Engraved Stone Gifting Assessment (October 2026)

Prepared as a market assessment and 14-day validation plan. Figures marked **(est.)** are my working assumptions, not researched facts. Check them against real quotes before you rely on them.

---

## 0. The short version

- **The idea is not "brilliant", and it is not dead either.** Engraved stone with a logo on it is a commodity. Engraved stone that is personal to the person receiving it, made in NZ and delivered for the buyer is a real but small service business.
- **This will not become a $100M company by itself.** The NZ settlement-gift market is roughly **NZ$3–10M a year in total** (worked out below). Corporate Christmas gifting is larger but seasonal and fought on price.
- **What makes it worth doing:** every settlement gift puts a Prime Piece object into the kitchen of someone who **has just bought a house**. Those people are exactly your furniture customers, and the real estate agent pays for that introduction. Treat gifting as a **paid customer-acquisition channel and a recurring cash engine for the furniture brand**, not as a separate company.
- **Your Jordan analogy, corrected:** the "Jordans" are not one big company. They are **real estate agents**. Each one is a local celebrity with a marketing budget and a need to be remembered. Prime Piece is the "Intel Inside": the agent's name goes on the gift, and your brand goes into the home.

### Underserved gaps

1. **Gifts personalised for each recipient, at agent volume.** Catalogue suppliers do "same logo × 100". Nobody local does "a different address and family name every week, delivered in 5 days".
2. **Builder handover gifts.** There are 41k consents a year, and group builders hand over keys constantly. The usual handover gift is wine or a hamper. There is no visible stone competitor.
3. **NZ-made natural stone at a premium price.** Pounamu owns the cultural story. Marble and granite in "made in Auckland, heavy, permanent" form is open territory.

### Rough market size (honest maths)

- Settlement gifts NZ: 80,655 sales × maybe 30–60% gifted **(est.)** × NZ$100–150 ≈ **NZ$2.4–7M a year**, across all gift types. Even a dominant 10% share is about **NZ$250–700k in revenue**.
- Australia's transaction volume is several times NZ's, so the same model there is a $20–40M category. That would still be a niche, not $100M.
- **Conclusion:** settlement gifting can produce a solid $300k–$1M ANZ revenue line at ~50% gross margin. It is a good small business and a great acquisition channel. It is not the $100M company.

## 3. Is corporate logo engraving valuable or a commodity?

**On its own, it's a commodity. Here is the evidence:**
- Logo on marble is available at **$18–29 a unit** from catalogue suppliers ([Sense2](https://sense2.com/product/18037)) and **US$15** for a paperweight ([PPI](https://promotionalproductinc.com/chisel-edged-paperweight)).
- Logo on wood is $40–68 delivered to NZ in 1–3 days ([Personalised Favours](https://personalisedfavours.com.au/products/engraved-rectangle-cheese-chopping-board-corporate-gift)).
- Buyers compare on price per head. You cannot beat imported marble on cost with local labour.

**What isn't a commodity, and what you should sell instead:**
1. **Personalisation for each recipient.** The client's name, address or date goes on the piece, and the company logo is small, on the underside or on the card. People keep a gift with *their* name on it. They put a gift with *your* logo on it in a drawer.
2. **Provenance and weight.** Real NZ-cut stone, offcuts given a second life, a numbered certificate. That story is worth $60–100 more than imported marble.
3. **Service.** The buyer sends a name, and you handle design, proof, gift wrapping and delivery to the recipient. That service is what the agent is paying for.

Previous clients (Body Bomb, Ugly Face, Bergs Digital) **prove you can make it, not that there is demand**. A one-off order is not evidence that customers come back. The real question is whether any of them would reorder without you chasing them.

## 4. The overlooked opportunity: the "Home Stone"

**A natural-stone address stone or house-number plaque, given at key handover by group builders, developers and top agents.**

Why it may beat everything above:
- **It's permanent and visible from the street.** A board lives in a kitchen cupboard. A house stone is seen by every neighbour, visitor and future buyer, so it markets the gift-giver, Prime Piece and the house.
- **It has two buyers.** It is a B2B gift (builder or agent) and a B2C product (homeowners upgrading their entrance). NZ buyers already pay **NZ$189** for a custom steel house number ([Lisa Sarah](https://www.lisasarah.com/products/handwritten-modern-house-number)) and NZ$330–650 for cast plaques.
- **It's highly standardised.** It uses one blank size, one font system and variable text only. That makes it ideal for outsourcing.
- **The volume driver is growing:** 41,268 consents in the year to Aug 2026, +21% ([PlaceMakers](https://underconstruction.placemakers.co.nz/consents-pass-40k-for-first-time-since-2023/)). Group builders hand over hundreds of homes a year each, so one builder contract matters more than 20 agents.
- **It's also the most plausible route to a scalable consumer brand.** "The house stone" as a category could be sold DTC in AU and the US through online ads, unlike custom furniture.

The risks: it's an outdoor product, so check durability, sealing and stone choice (granite, not marble). Builders' buying cycles are slow. Validate it in parallel with the settlement gift, not before it.

## 6. Taking yourself out of production

You get there in stages, and each stage takes a task off you.

1. **Standardise (month 1).** Offer 1 product, 2 stones, 1 size, 1 font family and 1 layout template. The customer chooses text only. There are no custom designs under 25 units.
2. **Automate ordering (month 1–2).** Agents get an account page (Shopify B2B or a form on primepiece.co.nz). The agent enters names, address, date and delivery details. A script generates the engraving file and a PDF proof automatically, and the agent clicks approve. You never touch the design.
3. **Batch production (month 2).** Pre-cut and polish 30–50 blanks during quiet periods. Engrave once a week in one batch. Change the promise to "delivered within 7 days" so batching works.
4. **Contract manufacturing (month 3+).** Agree a **written per-unit price** with Granite Workshop or another stone shop (there are dozens in Auckland). You pay per piece, not per hour, and you are not their employee doing side jobs.
5. **Separate the blank from the engraving (month 6+).** Buy pre-finished blanks in volume, from an NZ fabricator's offcuts or imported from stone exporters in India, Vietnam or Turkey. Do the variable engraving yourself with a laser (stone takes a laser mark well). A laser costs a few thousand dollars and one part-time person can run it. Price the laser before you buy it.
6. **Fulfilment.** Hand pick-pack-courier to a part-timer or a 3PL. Your job is selling to agents and builders and building the brand.

**Before any of this:** talk to your employer. You'd be using Granite Workshop's stone, machines and relationships. Get a clear written agreement covering who owns what, the pricing, and any conflict of interest with their own clients. If you skip this, you risk losing the job and the supplier at the same time.

## 9. Is this the best use of your time? (Honest challenge)

**The case for spending the time on furniture instead:**
- One table at NZ$1,500–3,500 or one plinth up to NZ$7,750 can earn as much gross profit as **15–40 boards**. If you have warm furniture leads you're not following up, chasing $119 boards is a mistake.
- You have limited hours. Gifting adds a second sales motion (B2B, many small accounts) next to your first (B2C, a few big tickets). Running both is how 22-year-olds burn out.

**The case for gifting:**
- Furniture is custom, heavy, slow and has no repeat purchases. It won't run without you. Gifting can be made into a system (section 6).
- **The gift is marketing for the furniture.** Every board goes to a household that just bought a house and is about to spend on furnishing it. Put a QR code and a "Prime Piece homeowner – 10% off your first piece" card in every box. If 1 in 100 gift recipients buys a table, the gift channel is paying *you* to acquire furniture customers.

**Decision rule:** keep at least **70% of your Prime Piece hours on furniture sales** during the test. If gifting passes go/no-go **and** at least one furniture enquiry traces back to a gift within 90 days, it has earned more of your time. If not, drop it without regret.

**On $100M:** that outcome needs a **consumer product that sells without you in markets bigger than NZ** (a DTC category like the "Home Stone", or a design label in the Marble Basics / En Gold mould sold through retailers). Gifting builds the cash, the systems and the recipient list to get there. It is not the destination.

## 10. Strongest recommendation and the first three actions

**Recommendation:** Launch the **Prime Piece Settlement Board** as a standardised, per-recipient-personalised gift sold to Auckland's top real estate agents on a recurring basis. Do not sell logo engraving as a product in its own right.
